# The Kept Week

A visual philosophy for Kinmova, written before the logo and share image were drawn.

The whole system rests on one unit: a filled circle, one per day. Seven of them make a week, and the week is the only pattern the brand ever repeats. A circle is the oldest mark of attendance there is, the tick on a register or the hole in a class card. Here it's made flat, solid and exact, with no outline, no shading and no sparkle. A day is either filled or it isn't.

Colour carries the meaning before any word does. Black is a day in the room with the class. Magenta is a day someone trained alone, between classes, which is the day the brand exists for. Pale sky is a rest day, present but quiet. Sand is the paper everything sits on. No other colour is allowed to compete. The proportions stay disciplined: mostly ground, a firm amount of ink, and magenta used sparingly, so it always reads as the thing worth noticing.

Scale is the main instrument. The same seven circles appear at the size of a full-stop beside the name, at the size of a fist across a page, and big enough to fill a room. Nothing about the arrangement changes as they grow, only the size, so the pattern is recognised at any distance. Spacing between the circles is a fixed fraction of their diameter, measured and held, because an even rhythm is what turns seven dots into a week.

Type is one family set with care: a round, even grotesque whose bowls echo the circles, tightened at display sizes and left open at reading sizes. Words are few and plain. The name is set in lowercase so it sits level with the dots rather than standing over them. Everything else gives way to the circles.

The finish matters as much as the idea. Every circle sits on the same baseline, every gap is the same to the pixel, every colour is matched exactly, and nothing is decorative. Each piece should feel patiently made, by someone at the top of their craft who checked every edge twice. The quiet is meant to look deliberate.
